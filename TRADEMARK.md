## What you may do without asking

1. **Refer to the project.** Use the name "Pentad" to say truthfully that something is Pentad, works with Pentad, or is based on Pentad: "a fork of Pentad", "a widget for Pentad", "packaged for Arch Linux".
2. **Distribute unmodified builds.** Linux distributions and other packagers may use the name and logo for builds of the project's released source with only the changes needed to build, package or integrate it (paths, dependency versions, distribution defaults). Please state your changes in the package description.
3. **Write about it.** Reviews, tutorials, screenshots and articles may use the name and logo to identify the project.

## What you may not do

1. **Pass off a modified version as the project.** If you distribute a version with functional or visual changes beyond item 2 above, rename it and remove the Pentad logo and any branding that could suggest it is the official project, for example "Pentad Pro", "Pentad Plus" or "Official Pentad".
2. **Imply endorsement.** Do not suggest that the maintainers sponsor, endorse or support your product, fork, service or distribution unless they have said so.
3. **Use the marks in your own identity.** Do not use "Pentad" or a confusingly similar name in a company, product, domain, package or account name for something that is not this project.
